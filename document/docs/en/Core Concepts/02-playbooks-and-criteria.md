## What a Playbook is

A Playbook records one setup type. It is not a single position and it is not trading advice.

Treat a Playbook as your description of how you identify, execute, and review a repeatable opportunity.

## What criteria are

Criteria are checklist items inside a Playbook. They turn abstract experience into questions you can check one by one.

Good criteria answer:

- What must be true before entry?
- What conditions should block the trade?
- How should risk be limited?
- What guides exit or reduction?

## Confluence and Playbook

Confluence is a group of signals appearing together. A Playbook is your long-term structure for one setup type.

A position can link to a Playbook and to Confluence. They answer different questions:

- Playbook: which setup type does this trade belong to?
- Confluence: which signals appeared together at the time?

## Why link positions back

Playbooks need real samples. After positions link back to a Playbook, you can review:

- Which setups you trade repeatedly.
- Which criteria are often met.
- Which positions broke your own rules.
- Which setups need to be changed or retired.

> [!info]
> A Playbook without historical positions is only an idea. With samples, it becomes a system you can improve.

## Create and maintain

1. Start from repeated patterns in real positions.
2. Create a Playbook.
3. Write the setup description.
4. Add criteria.
5. Link related positions back to it.
6. Update criteria after weekly review.
